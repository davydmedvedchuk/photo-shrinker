How it works

Three steps

No settings to learn. The defaults make photos smaller. Some quality is lost too, so keep your originals.

1

Drop photos

Choose them, or drag a whole batch onto the page. JPG, PNG, WebP, HEIC and more.

2

Pick size

Longest side in pixels, a format, a quality. Or just say "under 200 KB".

3

Download

Save one by one, share from your phone, or take all of them as a ZIP or a PDF.

Formats

Most of what you have

Photos from an iPhone, screenshots, old BMP files. It opens them and writes what you need.

In

JPGPNGWebPHEICBMPGIF

HEIC is the format iPhones use by default. It is converted right in the page.

Out

JPEGPNGWebPBMPICOZIPPDF

ICO makes favicons (up to 256 px). PDF puts each photo on its own A4 page.

Inside

Small things that matter

The parts that make it more than a resize button.

Max file size

Under 200 KB

Say how big a file may be. The app finds the best quality that fits, and shrinks the picture a little if it has to.

C++ · WebAssembly

Sharp resizing

The default scaler is a Lanczos resampler written in C++ and compiled to a 4 KB module. If it cannot load, the browser's own scaler takes over.

Python · command line

Whole folders

For hundreds of photos there is a small CLI, and a Windows exe built on GitHub.

$ shrink ./photos -s 1200 -m 200
FAQ

Questions

Are my photos uploaded?

No. The page does all the work on your device. There is no server that receives them.

What does it cost?

Nothing. No account, no ads. The code is open under the MIT licence.

Is metadata removed?

Yes. Photos are re-encoded, so EXIF data such as location is dropped. The rotation is kept.

Can I undo the shrinking?

No, lost detail does not come back. Keep your originals and send the small copies.

Shrink the next batch.

It takes a few seconds, and nothing leaves your device.
